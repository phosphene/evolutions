1. Identity: $U(s, s) = \mathbf{I}$

## 2. Mathematical Genealogy of Non-Autonomous Evolution & the Magnus Expansion

### 2.1 The Non-Commutativity Barrier & Time-Ordered Exponentials
When the generator $A(t)$ is time-dependent, generators at different time instances generally do not commute:
$$[A(t_1), A(t_2)] = A(t_1) A(t_2) - A(t_2) A(t_1) \neq \mathbf{0} \quad \text{for } t_1 \neq t_2$$

Consequently, the naive time-integral exponential fails:
$$\exp\left(\int_s^t A(\tau)^T d\tau\right) \neq U(t, s) \equiv \mathcal{T} \exp\left(\int_s^t A(\tau)^T d\tau\right)$$
where $\mathcal{T}$ denotes the Dyson time-ordering operator (Dyson 1949). Evaluating Dyson series directly suffers from secular terms and breaks geometric and algebraic invariants (such as unit trace and positivity).

### 2.2 The Magnus Expansion (Magnus 1954; Blanes et al. 2009)
Wilhelm Magnus (1954) proved that the linear differential equation $\dot{Y}(t) = M(t) Y(t), Y(0) = \mathbf{I}$ can be expressed locally as a single exponential of a Lie-algebraic series:
$$Y(t) = \exp(\Omega(t)), \quad \Omega(t) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \Omega_k(t)$$
where each $\Omega_k(t)$ is an element of the Lie algebra generated by $\{M(\tau) : 0 \le \tau \le t\}$.

For a time step $h = t - s$:
* **First term (Order $h$):**
  $$\Omega_1(s, h) = \int_0^h M(s + \tau) d\tau$$
* **Second term (Order $h^2$):**
  $$\Omega_2(s, h) = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^h d\tau_1 \int_0^{\tau_1} d\tau_2 \, [M(s + \tau_1), M(s + \tau_2)]$$

## 3. Concrete Numerical Quadratures: Magnus-2 and Magnus-4

Following Blanes et al. (2009 §3), we replace continuous integrals with Gauss–Legendre quadrature nodes to yield high-order, unitary/stochastic-invariant Lie-algebraic steppers for $M(t) = A(t)^T$:

### 3.1 Order 2: Midpoint Magnus (`magnus2`)
Using the 1-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature node $c_1 = \frac{1}{2}$:
$$\Omega^{[2]}(s, h) = h A\left(s + \frac{h}{2}\right)^T + \mathcal{O}(h^3)$$
Local truncation error is $\mathcal{O}(h^3)$, yielding global order 2 ($\mathcal{O}(h^2)$).

### 3.2 Order 4: Gauss–Legendre 2-Point Magnus (`magnus4`)
Using the symmetric 2-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature nodes:
$$c_1 = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}, \quad c_2 = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}$$
Evaluate the generator transpose at the quadrature points:
$$M_1 = A\left(s + c_1 h\right)^T, \quad M_2 = A\left(s + c_2 h\right)^T$$
The 4th-order Magnus generator is:
$$\Omega^{[4]}(s, h) = \frac{h}{2} (M_1 + M_2) + \frac{\sqrt{3} h^2}{12} [M_2, M_1]$$
where $[M_2, M_1] = M_2 M_1 - M_1 M_2$.
Local truncation error is $\mathcal{O}(h^5)$, yielding global order 4 ($\mathcal{O}(h^4)$).

## 4. Preservation of Physical Invariants & Literature Grounded Countermeasures

In continuous time, Markov jump generators satisfy two fundamental invariants:
1. **Conservative Flux (Zero Row Sums):** $\sum_j q_{ij}(t) = 0 \iff Q(t) \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0}$. (In transpose form for column distribution vectors: $\mathbf{1}^T A(t)^T = \mathbf{0}^T$).
2. **Metzler Non-Negativity:** $q_{ij}(t) \ge 0$ for $i \neq j$.

### 4.1 Invariant 1: Lie Bracket Preserves Conservative Flux Identically
Let $B_1, B_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$ be two conservative generators in column-form, meaning $\mathbf{1}^T B_1 = \mathbf{0}^T$ and $\mathbf{1}^T B_2 = \mathbf{0}^T$.
The Lie bracket is $[B_2, B_1] = B_2 B_1 - B_1 B_2$.
Evaluating the column sum:
$$\mathbf{1}^T [B_2, B_1] = \mathbf{1}^T (B_2 B_1 - B_1 B_2) = (\mathbf{1}^T B_2) B_1 - (\mathbf{1}^T B_1) B_2 = \mathbf{0}^T B_1 - \mathbf{0}^T B_2 = \mathbf{0}^T$$

**Theorem:** *The Lie algebra of conservative Markov generators is closed under the matrix commutator. The Magnus generator $\Omega(s, h)$ has column sums identically equal to zero for all orders.*

### 4.2 Invariant 2: Step-Size Control & Convergence Radius (Moan 2002; Blanes et al. 2009)
While the linear combination $\frac{h}{2}(M_1 + M_2)$ is strictly Metzler (off-diagonals $\ge 0$), the commutator term $[M_2, M_1]$ may have negative off-diagonals of order $\mathcal{O}(h^2)$.

Instead of inventing an ad-hoc heuristic or clipping matrix entries:
1. **Theoretical Convergence Radius:** The Magnus expansion converges absolutely in any matrix norm if:
   $$\int_s^{s+h} \|A(\tau)\| d\tau < \pi \approx 3.14159$$
   (Moan 2002; Casas 2007).
2. **Literature-Standard Step-Size Selection:**
   The commutator norm provides an intrinsic error estimator:
   $$\text{err}_{\text{comm}} \approx \frac{\sqrt{3} h^2}{12} \|[M_2, M_1]\|$$
   For stiff or rapidly varying generators where high-frequency driving causes off-diagonal sign changes, step size $h$ is segmented such that the leading Metzler term dominates the commutator:
   $$h \le \frac{6}{\sqrt{3}} \frac{\min_{i \ne j} (M_1 + M_2)_{ij}}{\max_{i \ne j} |[M_2, M_1]_{ij}|}$$

### 4.3 Invariant 3: Simplex Restoration via Euclidean Projection (Wang & Carreira-Perpiñán 2013)
Even when $\Omega$ is an exact generator, evaluating the matrix exponential under finite-precision arithmetic (via Al-Mohy & Higham 2009 Padé scaling and squaring) introduces rounding error $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\text{mach}})$, occasionally producing subnormal negative probabilities ($p_i \approx -10^{-16}$).

Following the proven Phase 1 primitive in `phosphene.evolutions.core.simplex`, the output of the matrix exponential is passed through the exact $O(K \log K)$ Euclidean projection onto the probability simplex $\Delta^K$ (Wang & Carreira-Perpiñán 2013). This completely eliminates negative probabilities without ad-hoc clamping or heuristic rescaling.
