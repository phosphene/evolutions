# Mathematical Genealogy, Numerical Compromises, and Tractable Units of Reason in `phosphene-evolutions`

## 1. The Core Abstraction: Two-Parameter Evolution Families on Banach Spaces

In infinite-dimensional analysis and dynamical systems (Kato 1953; Tanabe 1960; Pazy 1983; Engel & Nagel 2000), an **evolution family** (or evolution system) $\{U(t, s)\}_{t \ge s}$ on a Banach space $(X, \|\cdot\|)$ formalizes the propagation of states across non-autonomous intervals.

The mathematical definition rests upon two foundational axioms:
1. **Identity at Zero Elapsed Time:**
   $$U(s, s) = \mathbf{I} \quad \forall s \in \mathbb{R}$$
2. **Two-Parameter Semi-Flow Composition Law:**
   $$U(t, r) \circ U(r, s) = U(t, s) \quad \forall t \ge r \ge s$$

Under mild regularity conditions (strong continuity), the two-parameter family is generated by a family of closed, densely defined linear operators $\{A(t)\}_{t \ge 0}$ via the non-autonomous Cauchy problem:
$$\frac{d}{dt} x(t) = A(t) x(t), \quad x(s) = x_s \in \mathcal{D}(A(s)) \implies x(t) = U(t, s) x_s$$

### Dual Observables and Adjoint Evolution
Every forward evolution family $U(t, s) \in \mathcal{B}(X)$ naturally induces a backward dual evolution family $U^*(s, t) \in \mathcal{B}(X^*)$ on the continuous dual space $X^*$ through the canonical pairing:
$$\langle U(t, s) x, \phi \rangle_{X \times X^*} = \langle x, U^*(s, t) \phi \rangle_{X \times X^*} \quad \forall x \in X, \; \phi \in X^*, \; t \ge s$$
The composition of the dual family reverses parameter ordering:
$$U^*(s, r) \circ U^*(r, t) = U^*(s, t) \quad (s \le r \le t)$$

## 2. The Inevitable Numerical Compromises: Algebra vs. Silicon

The continuous functional calculus of operators on Banach spaces assumes infinite precision, exact continuum limits, and unconstrained dimensional scaling. When implemented on finite-precision floating-point hardware (IEEE 754 arithmetic), pure mathematical algebra collides with physical constraints. 

To maintain scientific integrity, a numerical library must explicitly document **where, why, and how numerical methods compromise pure algebraic purity**.

### Compromise 1: Infinite Dimensionality vs. Finite Grid Truncation (Boundary Flux Reflection)
* **The Algebra:** In continuous Markov jump processes (e.g. population lineages, queues, chemical master equations), the state space is countably infinite: $X = \ell^1(\mathbb{N}_0)$. The generator matrix $\mathbf{Q}$ is infinite-dimensional.
* **The Silicon Reality:** Arrays in computer memory must be truncated to a finite dimension $K < \infty$.
* **The Compromise:** Naive truncation sets $q_{ij} = 0$ for $j > K$, which causes probability mass arriving at state $K$ to vanish into an unmodeled absorbing sink:
  $$\sum_{j=0}^K q_{Kj} = -q_{K, K+1} < 0 \implies \frac{d}{dt} \sum_{i=0}^K p_i(t) < 0$$
* **The Phosphene Resolution (`ZeroFluxMetzler`):** We enforce reflective boundary closure at state $K$. The forward exit rate $q_{K, K+1}$ is explicitly folded back onto the diagonal $q_{KK} = -\sum_{j < K} q_{Kj}$, guaranteeing that the truncated row sum $\sum_{j=0}^K q_{ij} \equiv 0$ holds identically. 
  * *Trade-off:* We sacrifice modeling unbounded excursions in exchange for exact probability conservation ($\|p(t)\|_1 = 1.0$) and preventing catastrophic floating-point renormalization loops.

### Compromise 2: Padé Rational Approximants vs. Positivity Preservation
* **The Algebra:** The operator exponential $T(\tau) = \exp(\tau \mathbf{Q}) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{(\tau \mathbf{Q})^k}{k!}$ of a Metzler matrix ($\mathbf{Q}$ with $q_{ij} \ge 0$ for $i \neq j$) is mathematically guaranteed to be a positive operator:
  $$p \ge 0 \implies \exp(\tau \mathbf{Q}) p \ge 0 \quad \forall \tau \ge 0$$
* **The Silicon Reality:** Direct Taylor summation is numerically unstable for stiff operators (Moler & Van Loan 1978, 2003). Modern scientific runtimes (including `scipy.linalg.expm`) utilize diagonal $[m/m]$ Padé rational approximants coupled with scaling and squaring (Al-Mohy & Higham 2009):
  $$\exp(\tau \mathbf{Q}) \approx \left[ R_{mm}\left( 2^{-s} \tau \mathbf{Q} \right) \right]^{2^s}, \quad R_{mm}(Z) = [D_{mm}(Z)]^{-1} N_{mm}(Z)$$
* **The Compromise:** Padé rational functions $R_{mm}(Z)$ contain negative coefficients in their partial fraction expansions. Consequently, floating-point subtraction and cancellation errors inevitably produce tiny negative probabilities ($p_i \approx -10^{-16}$ to $-10^{-14}$) in empty or rarely visited states.
* **The Phosphene Resolution (`project_to_simplex`):** Rather than using heuristic ad-hoc clamping (`p = np.maximum(p, 0); p /= p.sum()`), which distorts the probability gradient and destroys log-likelihood curvature, we solve the exact $O(n \log n)$ Euclidean projection onto the probability simplex $\Delta^K$ (Wang & Carreira-Perpiñán 2013).
  * *Trade-off:* We incur an $O(n \log n)$ sort per propagation step to restore physical positivity without uncalibrated mass distortion.

### Compromise 3: The Stiffness Barrier vs. Time Discretization (Padé vs. Uniformization)
* **The Algebra:** Both Padé exponential integration and Poisson uniformization represent the exact same continuous semigroup operator $T(\tau) = \exp(\tau \mathbf{Q})$.
* **The Silicon Reality:**
  1. *Stiffness Ratio:* Continuous systems often feature disparate timescales. The spectral stiffness ratio $S_R = \frac{|\operatorname{Re}(\omega_{\max})|}{|\operatorname{Re}(\omega_{\min \neq 0})|}$ can exceed $10^6$.
  2. *Jensen Uniformization (Grassmann 1977; Gross & Miller 1984):* Expands flow into a Poisson sum of discrete transitions:
     $$P(t + \tau) = \sum_{j=0}^\infty e^{-\gamma \tau} \frac{(\gamma \tau)^j}{j!} \mathbf{P}_{\text{disc}}^j P(t), \quad \gamma = \max_i |q_{ii}|, \quad \mathbf{P}_{\text{disc}} = \mathbf{I} + \frac{1}{\gamma} \mathbf{Q}^T$$
     *Advantage:* $\mathbf{P}_{\text{disc}}$ and Poisson weights are strictly non-negative. Zero negative probability can ever be generated; simplex projection is completely unnecessary.
     *Failure Mode:* When $\alpha = \gamma \tau \gg 1$ (high stiffness or long duration), the Poisson expansion requires $J \sim \mathcal{O}(\gamma \tau)$ dense matrix-vector multiplications, rendering computation prohibitively slow or causing floating-point underflow in Poisson weight accumulation.
  3. *Padé Scaling-and-Squaring:* Scales matrix norm $\|2^{-s} \tau \mathbf{Q}\| \le \theta_m$, requiring only $\mathcal{O}(\log_2(\gamma \tau))$ matrix squarings.
     *Advantage:* Flawless asymptotic stability and rapid evaluation for stiff operators across large $\tau$.
     *Failure Mode:* Roundoff negativity requires geometric simplex projection.
* **The Phosphene Resolution:** We provide both propagators as calibrated, tractable alternatives under a unified signature. We equip generators with an explicit `audit_generator_stiffness` tool to diagnose when uniformization is superior (small $\gamma \tau$, zero-negativity critical) versus when Padé scaling-and-squaring is required (stiff $\gamma \tau \gg 1$).
